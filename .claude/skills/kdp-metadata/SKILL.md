# /kdp-metadata — Stage 8: DESCRIPTION AND BACKEND KEYWORD RESEARCH

## MANDATORY RESEARCH GATE — COMPLETE THIS FIRST
Live web research is compulsory and unconditional. It must be performed before any description or keyword is written. It does not depend on browser control being available and may not be skipped, softened, deferred or replaced with prior knowledge.
1. Make at least one genuine web search or browsing tool call in this session before anything else. Prioritise Amazon.com (United States, Books or Kindle Store department; for fr/de/es books the matching marketplace in that language) and Google.
2. Use the owner's connected browser when one is available and let the owner watch. Before browser actions follow the permission mode the owner chooses (ask once for the whole plan, ask before every action, or no browser). Tell the owner in plain words what you are about to do, do it live, then say briefly what you found. Sequence: open Amazon; (after permission) set the delivery location to a general US ZIP such as 90001 and confirm what shows; select Books or Kindle Store; for each important seed term type it into the search bar so the autocomplete suggestions appear and note the relevant ones; open each promising results page; for nonfiction open the relevant bestseller categories and subcategories (not only a general page); run the relevant Google searches. Never sign in, never buy, never change account settings or take irreversible actions.
3. Put a "Research Proof" block at the very top of the final output: today's date, the plain-language log of every approved browser action and the pages actually visited, every search query, and 3 to 8 specific findings with their sources. Record only what happened.
4. If no web-search or browsing tool is available at all, stop and reply exactly: "I cannot complete this task as specified because no live web-search or browsing tool is enabled in this session. Please run me in a Claude surface with web access." Do not proceed from prior knowledge.
5. Do not write a single description or keyword before the Research Proof block exists and holds genuine results.
6. Never claim to have done any browser or search action that was not completed. Disclose limitations honestly and prominently.

## Compulsory name and trademark exclusion
Do not include any person's name, author's name, competitor's name, public figure's name, company, organisation, brand, product, book-series name, trademark, trademarked word or phrase, registered slogan, protected catchphrase, proprietary term or recognisable branded expression in: any description, the final description, the readable versions, the KDP HTML versions, the keyword shortlist, or any of the seven backend keywords. This applies even when such a name appears in autocomplete, competitor listings, bestseller pages, Google results or popular terminology. Research may examine competitors for market understanding but their names and protected wording must never transfer. No altered spellings, abbreviations, initials, plurals, hidden variations or indirect references to imitate a protected name. No phrases like "like [author]", "for readers of [title]", "[brand] method", "[trademarked term] guide", "similar to [competitor]". Use only generic, natural, descriptive, manuscript-relevant language. Verify uncertain terms before using them; do not assume a word is safe because it is frequent on Amazon or Google. Reject and replace any doubtful phrase.

## Role
Amazon KDP book-description specialist, manuscript analyst, metadata researcher and publishing copywriter. Base every output on the manuscript and on current public marketplace research you actually performed. Do not invent information, benefits, topics, qualifications, results, statistics, endorsements, keyword metrics, search volumes, rankings or browser activity.

## Inputs
The complete manuscript (`final/<slug>-manuscript.docx` or the chapter Markdown files), `book.yaml`, `market-research.md` (positioning and gaps), `title-check.md`. Do not ask for the cover, an existing description, keywords, categories, audience, reading age or publication status; determine them from the manuscript. Use the title and subtitle from `book.yaml`.

## Procedure

### 1. Read the entire manuscript and analyse it
Determine: main subject and purpose; the central problem or need; the genuine value proposition; what the reader will understand or be able to apply; the topics actually covered; genre, niche, tone, depth, complexity; the target reader and their needs, frustrations, expectations and search intent; distinctive features; the strongest accurate selling points; the manuscript's own terminology; any limitations that must not be misrepresented. Use no claim that cannot be verified from the manuscript. Promise no guaranteed success, transformation, income, health improvement, professional advancement or academic achievement.

### 2. Live research (after the gate above)
- Extract seed terms from the manuscript: main subject, chapter topics, reader problems, questions answered, methods, intended readers, reader goals, professional and academic terminology, applications, alternative phrasings, genre and category language, tools or frameworks genuinely discussed, phrases used throughout. Do not start from random popular keywords.
- For each important seed term: enter it into the Amazon search bar, note relevant native autocomplete suggestions, test words before and after, singular/plural, audience, problem and application variants, beginner/advanced/professional/handbook/workbook/reference variants only where they fit; search the most promising phrases; examine the books in the results; confirm they match the manuscript and reader; reject phrases returning unrelated products; record useful phrases and their evidence. Never rely on autocomplete alone; check each phrase against the manuscript, real results, relevant books, reader intent and KDP metadata policies. Autocomplete does not prove volume, profitability, low competition, sales, ranking or buyer intent; do not claim it does.
- Study relevant bestseller categories and books for common terminology, recurring reader problems, wording in titles and subtitles, how target readers are named, topic combinations, long-tail patterns, practical applications and search-result gaps that genuinely fit the manuscript. Do not copy another book's title, subtitle, description, author name or branding. A term is not chosen merely because a bestseller uses it; relevance to this manuscript is the primary criterion.
- Optional legitimate suggestion-expansion tools may be used if installed; never claim they reveal Amazon's private algorithm, exact volume or sales.
- Cross-check key findings with Google results, official sources and the current KDP guidance on descriptions, keywords and categories (open the live KDP help pages; record the current description character limit and keyword field rules).

### 3. Descriptions
Each description is about 350 to 550 words, never exceeding the current KDP character limit including HTML tags, and follows this compulsory structure: **Hook** (strong, honest opening tied to the manuscript) → **Problem** (the real difficulty or unmet need) → **Promise** (what the book helps the reader understand, apply or solve, no guaranteed outcome) → **Benefits** (four to seven substantial reader-centred benefits describing coverage or capability, not guaranteed results) → **Key Topics** (the most important subjects genuinely covered) → **Target Reader** (who it is genuinely for, from the manuscript) → **Strong Call to Action** (confident, direct, specific to this manuscript; no false urgency, pressure, scarcity, limited-time wording, prices, discounts, guaranteed outcomes or unsupported claims).
Produce at least three genuinely different descriptions: (1) Problem-Solution positioning, (2) Practical Benefits and Application positioning, (3) Comprehensive Guide or Authority positioning (more angles allowed if they fit). For each, give two versions: **Readable** (clean text for editorial review, back-cover adaptation, comparison) and **Amazon KDP HTML** (only necessary supported tags: p, br, b, strong, i, em, h4, h5, h6, ul, li; no CSS, JavaScript, tables, images, external links, unsupported or decorative HTML, excess bold; every tag opened and closed; same substance as the readable version).
Description restrictions: no guaranteed results, unverified statistics, fabricated achievements, false qualifications, invented awards, unsupported endorsements, reviews or testimonials, requests for reviews, competitor author names or titles, prices or discounts, website links, emails, phone numbers, addresses, alternative ordering instructions, time-sensitive promotion, keyword lists disguised as sentences, "number one", "best book", "bestseller" or "guaranteed success" claims unless independently verified and permitted, keyword stuffing, or information not in or reasonably following from the manuscript.

### 4. Compare and finalise
Compare the three on: hook strength, problem clarity, promise accuracy, benefit quality, topic coverage, target-reader identification, persuasiveness, readability, call-to-action strength, KDP compliance, appeal, faithfulness to the manuscript. Name the strongest overall, the strongest opening, the clearest benefits, the strongest call to action, the most suitable for Amazon, and which elements to combine. Then write ONE final optimised description combining the strongest accurate elements (follows the structure, detailed, strong opening, honest promise, 4 to 7 benefits, clear reader, manuscript-specific call to action, no repetition, no unsupported claims, within the character limit), in readable text and in KDP HTML.

### 5. Keywords
- Initial shortlist of at least 20 relevant phrases drawn from the manuscript, autocomplete, results pages, bestseller categories, comparable-book terminology (without names), reader problems and goals, long-tail patterns, professional or academic terms, applications. Evaluate each on: manuscript relevance, reader intent, search-result relevance, specificity, natural wording, buyer intent, distinctiveness, policy compliance, duplication with the title and subtitle, duplication with other candidates, whether it attracts the right reader, whether it is too broad, misleading or unrelated. Reject weak or irrelevant phrases even if autocomplete shows them.
- Select EXACTLY SEVEN backend keyword phrases: accurate, naturally searchable, relevant to the audience, a meaningful search pathway, specific rather than vague single words, not repeating the title or subtitle needlessly, no unrelated popular terms, no competitor, author or brand names, no unauthorised trademarks, no prices, promotions or temporary claims, none of "bestseller", "number one", "guaranteed", "free", "new release", compliant with current KDP metadata requirements (check each field's current length limit), suitable for direct entry into the seven fields. Describe them only as evidence-based recommendations, strongly supported search phrases, relevant keyword opportunities or potential buyer-intent phrases, never as guaranteed ranking keywords.
- **Keyword evidence table** for each of the seven: phrase; connection to the manuscript; target reader; likely search intent; Amazon autocomplete evidence; Amazon search-result evidence; bestseller or comparable-book evidence; reason for selection; potential weakness; confidence High / Medium / Low. When no autocomplete evidence exists, say so and name the alternative evidence. Never fabricate evidence.

### 6. Compliance check before delivery
Check every description and keyword for names, trademarks, protected phrases, unsupported claims and policy violations. Then run `/amazon-compliance` with scopes `description` and `keywords`. Include the KDP compliance checklist in the deliverable.

## Compulsory deliverable: a Word document
Write `final/KDP-metadata.docx` (a chat-only answer is not acceptable; build it with the docx skill or pandoc from a Markdown file, clear headings, copy-ready sections). Contents in order: (1) Research Proof block; (2) title and subtitle; (3) manuscript analysis summary; (4) target reader; (5) main reader problem; (6) value proposition; (7) key manuscript topics; (8 to 13) Description One, Two, Three each in readable text and in KDP HTML; (14) comparison; (15) recommended final description, readable; (16) recommended final description, KDP HTML; (17) initial keyword shortlist; (18) exactly seven recommended backend keyword phrases; (19) keyword evidence table; (20) research record (date, marketplace, ZIP or location, department, bestseller categories examined, seed terms tested, autocomplete suggestions collected, tools used, comparable books examined, shortlist, accepted and rejected phrases with reasons, final seven, official KDP guidance consulted, limitations); (21) Amazon KDP compliance checklist; (22) research limitations and disclosures.
Do NOT include ideas, titles, outlines or concepts for another or future book; no "next book idea"; no suggestions of sequels, companion books, workbooks, journals or revised editions unless the owner asks. Do not mark the task complete until the DOCX exists and has been provided.

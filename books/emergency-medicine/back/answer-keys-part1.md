Part 1 answer key (goes to Appendix A at assembly)

1. B. Scene safety comes before patient contact because an injured responder becomes a second patient.
2. D. Triage orders patients by how soon they must be seen to stay safe, not by arrival, volume of complaint or expected stay.
3. A. A level is a starting point; a patient who deteriorates is reassessed, moved to a higher level and escalated.
4. C. Blood pressure can stay normal for a time in shock, so cool, pale skin and fast breathing still call for urgent attention.
5. D. A patient who responds only to a loud voice is rated V (voice).
6. A. A pulse oximeter does not show how well a patient is breathing out carbon dioxide, so a normal reading can coexist with poor ventilation.
7. C. Findings in survey order, the vital signs and what you did belong in Assessment.
8. Model answer: (1) Check the scene for hazards and protect yourself. (2) Take the first look and assess responsiveness. (3) Call for help early, because fast breathing and a reduced response to voice suggest the patient may worsen. (4) Assess airway and breathing and count the breathing rate. Then continue through circulation, disability and exposure.

Part 2 answer key (goes to Appendix A at assembly)

1. B. No air at the mouth with opposite chest and abdomen movement means complete obstruction with strong effort to breathe.
2. A. The jaw thrust moves the neck less than the head-tilt chin-lift.
3. D. Suction is used only when fluid is seen or heard, in short bursts.
4. C. A conscious adult with a severe blockage needs back blows and abdominal thrusts in the order your training and protocol teach; compressions begin only if the person becomes unresponsive.
5. A. Over-squeezing or giving breaths too fast can push air into the stomach and cause vomiting.
6. D. A patient who has stopped struggling but remains blue and unable to speak may be tiring and failing.
7. B. Call emergency services and help the patient use their own epinephrine auto-injector; delay is dangerous. Giving medicines is outside many roles; follow your scope and medical direction.
8. Model answer: (1) Make the scene safe and call for help early. (2) Check airway and breathing and assess breathing rate, effort, words per breath and mental state. (3) Place the patient in the position that is easiest for breathing, usually upright. (4) Give oxygen only as your scope and medical direction allow. Oxygen needs care because in some patients with long-term lung disease a high concentration of oxygen can make breathing less effective, so the target is set by protocol or medical direction; do not withhold oxygen from a patient who needs it. Drowsiness is a warning of failing breathing. Giving medicines is outside many roles; follow your scope and medical direction.

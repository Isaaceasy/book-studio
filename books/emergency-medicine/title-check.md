# Title Check: Emergency Medicine (working title)

Date: 2026-10-29. Marketplace: amazon.com, English/US. Positioning approved at Stage 1: complete student reference in one volume (nursing, paramedic/EMT, allied health), color, target $39.99 to $49.99.

## 1. What was screened
Main title: "Emergency Medicine" (owner's working title). Subtitle: none yet. Series name: none. No made-up words, brand names, person names or franchise phrases in the title.

## 2. Searches and registries actually consulted
- WebSearch: "Emergency Medicine" trademark registered mark books publications USPTO. Result: no federal registration for "Emergency Medicine" alone surfaced. It surfaced related marks on a trademark-mirror site (Justia): EMRA (a residents' association, Class 16 publications, listed as registered and renewed) and YEAR BOOK OF EMERGENCY MEDICINE (registered 1985, renewed). The mirror can lag the official register.
- USPTO Trademark Search (tmsearch.uspto.gov): opened in the browser, but the search page showed no usable input in my session, so I could NOT run a search there. **UNVERIFIED.** Link for the owner to check: https://tmsearch.uspto.gov/search (search the phrase "emergency medicine" and the final title, classes 9, 16, 41).
- EUIPO, UK IPO and WIPO registries: NOT searched (US marketplace).
- WebSearch and KDP help pages for rules (section 3).
- Amazon (live, ZIP 90001): searched "emergency medicine" (3 result pages, 99 listings) and five alternative titles.

## 3. KDP rules read live (2026-10-29)
Source: KDP help "Title and subtitle" page (G201097560), read via WebFetch, and KDP help G201953870 shown in search results.
- Title and subtitle together must be fewer than 200 characters.
- Title field holds only the actual title as on the cover; subtitle follows the same rules and adds information about the content.
- Not allowed: repeating generic keywords, unauthorized references to other titles, authors or trademarked terms, implying contributors who did not contribute, sales-rank words such as "bestselling", promotional words such as "free", placeholder text, HTML tags.
- For print, title and subtitle must match the cover.
The page gave no separate keyword-stuffing rule for titles; I treat stuffing as a risk anyway.

## 4. Findings
- **Trademark:** "Emergency Medicine" is a plain descriptive phrase for the subject. Related marks exist (EMRA, YEAR BOOK OF EMERGENCY MEDICINE) that include the phrase as part of longer marks. Using the plain phrase as the subject of a book is normal, but the title must not copy or imitate those marks. Registry check incomplete, so UNVERIFIED.
- **Marketplace collision:** among 99 listings no book is titled exactly "Emergency Medicine". Eleven titles start with the words "Emergency Medicine" (for example Essentials, Secrets, Oral Board Review, Field Atlas, Procedures, Thinker, Decision Handbook). The bare phrase is crowded and sounds physician-level, while the readers here are nursing, paramedic and allied-health students.
- **Series and format phrases to avoid in the subtitle:** "Secrets", "Made Easy", "Made Incredibly Easy", "Made Ridiculously Simple", "at a Glance", "Case Files", "Pocket", "Basics of". These imitate established series.
- **Look-alike risk:** the first pages are full of 2026 indie titles with long keyword-heavy subtitles ("Rapid", "Clinical Encyclopedia", "ICU Protocols"). Do not copy that style.
- **Health wording:** the subtitle must not promise cure, treatment outcomes or replace training, medical direction or local protocol. No dose or drug claims (owner rule).

## 5. Risk level: UNVERIFIED (expected LOW for trademark, MEDIUM for discoverability)
Reason: the official US register could not be searched in this session; the phrase is descriptive and no exact-title book was seen, but the bare title is generic and crowded, so the subtitle has to carry the audience.

Character counts include "Emergency Medicine: " (title, colon, space) plus the subtitle. Compliance review (compliance/title.md) result: REVISE, no BLOCK. Option 1 passes unconditionally; options 3, 5 and 6 need re-review against the locked outline; option 4 has a double colon with the title; option 7's "Core Knowledge" may be a third-party mark (unchecked); option 2's "Management" may imply drug management.

Checked silently: none repeats "emergency" or "medicine" from the title; "students" repeats across options only. Option 7 uses "EMT" once, deliberately. Options 3, 5 and 6 depend on the final figure and procedure scope (50 figures and procedures are approved scope). Option 2's "Management" must match the content (procedures and care priorities, no dose figures).

## 7. Recommendation
**Option 3** is the strongest prediction: it names the two student groups that the Amazon box suggested ("paramedic books", "emergency nursing ..."), says what the book contains (assessment, priorities, procedures), and signals the visual feature that the gap analysis found missing in the $30 to $50 band. Option 1 is the cleanest and the best A/B partner. This is a reasoned prediction, not a guarantee of sales; performance depends on competition, reviews and testing. Compare options 3 and 1 before committing, for example by showing both mock covers to a few students. Do not swap the subtitle after launch without a revised cover: a print subtitle must match the cover.

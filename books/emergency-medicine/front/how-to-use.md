# How to Use This Book {data-label="Before you begin" .back .front}

This guide follows the order in which a responder thinks in an emergency: make the scene safe, sort who cannot wait, assess in a fixed order, then deal with the problem in front of you. It is written for nursing students, paramedic and EMT students, and allied health students in their first emergency placements or courses.

**Six Parts.** Part 1 teaches the order of thinking and is the one to read first. Parts 2 to 6 deal with airway and breathing, the heart and resuscitation, injury and the environment, medical emergencies, and special groups of patients. Each Part opens with a short list of what you will be able to do, gives its topics as numbered entries, and closes with an illustrative case, a summary of priorities and eight review questions. The answers are in Appendix A.

**Scope of practice.** What you may do depends on your license, certification, course and the rules of your service or school. Each procedure says so, and each tells you when to stop and call for help. When this book and your local protocol differ, follow your protocol.

**What the boxes mean.** *Safety* boxes warn of harm from a wrong action. *Red Flags* boxes list signs that call for urgent help. *Scope Note* boxes mark limits of role or training. *Remember* boxes give one memory aid. *Care Priorities* boxes close each Part with the tie-breaking rules for competing problems. *Illustrative Case* boxes are fictional.

**What is not here.** This book gives no medicine doses. It does not teach intravenous access, giving medicines, reading heart tracings or advanced airway skills. It is not a course, and it does not replace one. Practice each skill on a manikin, with an instructor, before you use it on a person.

Part 3 answer key (goes to Appendix A at assembly)

1. C. In a heart attack, blood flow to part of the muscle is cut off; in cardiac arrest the heartbeat has stopped being effective, and a heart attack may or may not lead to it.
2. A. Serious chest pain is treated as cardiac until a clinician shows otherwise, so you call early and keep the patient still; driving delays the call.
3. B. A flat line in a patient who is awake and talking is almost certainly a loose lead or electrode, so you read the patient first.
4. D. The recommended adult rate is 100 to 120 compressions a minute.
5. A. Everyone must be clear of the patient before the shock so that nobody receives it.
6. C. A fast heart rate with cool, pale skin is an early sign; warm hands with a strong pulse are not.
7. D. The body compensates early, so blood pressure may be normal while the patient is already in shock.
8. Model answer: (1) Check that the scene is safe, tap the shoulders and shout; he does not respond. (2) Call for help or have someone call emergency services and fetch the AED. (3) Look for breathing for no more than 10 seconds; gasping is not normal breathing, so treat it as cardiac arrest. (4) Begin compressions on the lower half of the breastbone at 100 to 120 a minute, at least 2 inches (5 centimeters) deep, letting the chest recoil. (5) When the AED arrives, switch it on, attach the pads, follow its prompts, make sure everyone is clear, and resume compressions at once after the shock or if none is advised. Compressions come first because they keep a little blood moving to the brain and heart while the AED is fetched.

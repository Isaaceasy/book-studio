Supersedes the title and subtitle sections of title-check.md (which screened "Emergency Medicine"). The older file is kept for the KDP rules and the trademark notes.

## Research done (amazon.com, ZIP 90001, built-in browser)
- Typed into the real search box: "emergency nursing e" suggested "emergency nursing essentials"; "emergency nursing book" suggested "emergency nursing book master the essential basics"; "nursing essentials" suggested only nursing-school supply phrases. Suggestions can be personalised and do not prove volume.
- Searched "emergency nursing essentials" (2 pages, 48 listings): NO book with that exact title. The listings were led by emergency-nursing titles already seen (a $31.30 indie, a leading manual, a core curriculum), a charting guide ($9.99 and $23.99), a certification study guide ($37.97) and a "first 100 shifts" guide ($14.99). The only "Essentials" title was a dental-professional emergencies book ($76.65).
- Not done: USPTO register search (owner to run https://tmsearch.uspto.gov/search for "emergency nursing essentials", classes 9, 16, 41). Risk stays UNVERIFIED, expected low-to-medium ("Essentials" is a common word used by several publisher series, so the title should not be styled like a series).

Option 2 and 7 avoid repeating "nursing" because the title already carries it; the subtitle adds the other two reader groups. Options 5 and 7 promise illustrated procedures and color, so they depend on the figures and color interior being delivered. No cure, treatment, outcome or "replaces training" wording; no "step-by-step" promise.

## Recommendation
**Option 2** (best discoverability balance: the title carries the nursing keyword, the subtitle adds paramedic and allied health, plus "Illustrated", "Assessment", "Procedures"). Option 1 is the safest and the best comparison. This is a reasoned prediction, not a guarantee of sales. A print subtitle must match the cover, so decide before the cover is made.
